What is Nira ?

Nira is a low-level system programming and Linux, non-profit community made by self-learners to help others who are in the same boat.

Nira was originally built by a group of Tamil people who wanted to create a place to learn and discuss programming together. But the community is not limited to Tamil people at all. Everyone is welcome here, regardless of where you are from. We mostly speak and discuss things in English, so English is the main language we use in the community.

We are not an NGO, a specialized professional community, or some "Learning C in 100 days" bootcamp community. Not at all. We are ourselves learners, so we are not a big community that does several seminars and weekly meetups. Nope. Even though we may have plans for things like that in the future, that is not what Nira is mainly about.

Our community is mainly built around learning and discussing topics related to low-level system programming. The reason for this is pretty simple: low-level system programming is a difficult field, and there are only a small number of communities that are really dedicated to it. We want to keep this space for people who are genuinely interested in learning and understanding how things work at a lower level.

Even though we are niche because of our focus on low-level system programming, the things we discuss are actually pretty broad. We cover programming languages such as:

  • C
  • C++
  • Rust
  • Zig
  • Odin

Beyond programming languages, we also discuss electronics, computer systems, computer philosophy, data structures and algorithms, performance and optimizations techniques, simulations, physics, maths and other deep technical things. If we want to be more specific, some of the topics we cover include:

  • Embedded systems
  • Compilers
  • Operating systems
  • GPGPU and parellel computing
  • Graphics programming
  • Game engine development
  • Network systems
  • Cybersecurity

We also talk about Linux and pretty much everything around it, including:

  • Linux distros
  • Desktop environments and window managers
  • Linux ricing and customization
  • Linux tools
  • FOSS and open-source software
  • Self-hosting
  • Linux-related workflows and experiments

The main idea is that we don't want to limit ourselves to one specific language or one specific field. Low-level system programming is our main focus, but there are many things connected to it, and we want people to be able to explore those things as they learn.

Everyone here is at a different stage of learning. Some may just be starting with programming, while others may already be going deeper into things like C, operating systems, compilers, embedded systems, Linux, or other areas. There is no specific level you need to be at to be part of the community. You can be a complete beginner or someone already working on advanced topics, as long as you have the interest to learn and understand things.

So, we are all basically in the same boat. Some people may know more about one topic while being completely new to another, and that's completely fine. Nira is just a place where we can learn, experiment, explore different areas, share what we find, and help each other along the way.

Before being part of our community, check out our policies to know more about the guidelines and what we expect from you guys. This helps us all understand how the community works, so we can improve together and make Nira a better community for everyone.

How Nira Works ?

The main pillars behind Nira are the contributors. Contributors are people who continuously contribute to the projects we are working on, support the community, engage with other community members, and help move things forward.

The contributions you make within Nira can stack over time. As you continue contributing and becoming more involved, you may eventually have the chance to become part of Nira's main team and take responsibility for different parts of the community and its projects. The idea is that responsibility can grow naturally as people become more involved and demonstrate that they are willing to contribute and help maintain the community.

If you want to contribute to one of our projects, make sure to check our GitHub repositories first. A project is generally open for contribution when it has a separate "Contributing" section alongside its README, or when contribution guidelines are provided directly in the README. If you find those guidelines, you can follow them to understand how the project accepts contributions and what you need to do before submitting one.

We want contribution to be something that people can participate in naturally. You don't need to be part of Nira's main team to contribute, and you don't need to contribute to every project. What matters is that people who choose to participate can actually help build, improve, and maintain the things that make up the community.
