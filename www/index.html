What is Nira?

Nira is a non-profit community for low-level system programming and Linux, built by self-learners for anyone who happens to be in the same boat.

Low-level system programming is the kind of programming that happens close to the hardware. Think operating systems, compilers, embedded devices, and code that talks to memory and the processor directly. It's difficult, it's unforgiving, and (sadly) there are only a handful of communities dedicated to it. Nira is our attempt at adding one more, a place for people who care about how things work under the hood and not just that they work.

We may be niche in focus, but certainly not in topics. On the language side, you'll find us talking about the following.

  • C
  • C++
  • Rust
  • Zig
  • Odin

Beyond languages, we also dig into electronics, computer systems, the philosophy of computing, data structures and algorithms, performance optimization, simulations, physics, maths, and a whole lot of other deeply technical things. To be a bit more specific, these are some of the areas we cover.

  • Embedded systems
  • Compilers
  • Operating systems
  • Graphics programming
  • Network systems
  • Cybersecurity

Then there's Linux, which is the other half of Nira, along with pretty much everything that orbits it.

  • Linux distros
  • Desktop environments and window managers
  • Linux ricing and customization
  • FOSS and open-source software
  • Self-hosting
  • Linux-related workflows and experiments

Low-level programming is the core, but it touches nearly everything above, and we'd rather have you follow those connections than stay locked inside a single box.

There's no level you need to be at to join. Some of us are writing our very first programs, others are knee-deep in compilers, kernels or firmware, and most are somewhere in between, an expert in one topic and a complete beginner in the next. That's fine, and honestly, that's the point. Nira is a place to learn, experiment, share what we find, and help each other along the way.

Before you join, please take a look at our policies. They explain how the community works and what we expect from its members, so we can all make Nira a better place together.

How Nira Works

Nira runs on contributions. Contributors are the people who keep showing up, whether that means working on our projects, helping other members, joining discussions, or simply pushing things forward.

Contributions stack up over time. Stay involved long enough and you may be invited to join the main team and take responsibility for parts of the community and its projects. We'd rather let that happen naturally, as people show that they're willing to help and look after the place, than hand out roles on day one.

Want to contribute to a project? Start with our GitHub repositories. A project is open for contributions when it has a separate "Contributing" section next to its README, or when the contribution guidelines are written in the README itself. Those guidelines tell you how the project accepts contributions and what you should do before submitting one.
