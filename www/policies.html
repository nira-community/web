Policies

1. No Vibe Coding

Vibe coding is when you describe what you want to an AI, let it write the code, and then judge the result by whether it works. You don't write most of it, and you don't really have to understand it either.

We're against it, and the reason is rather simple. It skips the part where you actually learn. Nira is about understanding low-level systems, and that understanding comes from reading, experimenting, getting things wrong, debugging, and eventually figuring out why something works. Vibe coding jumps straight to the output. Getting something to run is the smallest part of programming, and we're not willing to give up the rest just because a tool can now answer instantly.

That doesn't mean we hate AI. We use it too, for learning, debugging, explaining concepts, quick lookups, and rewording things so they read better. What we don't do is paste in raw generated code and call it a day. We still do the work of understanding and writing things ourselves.

If you want our two cents, using AI as a springboard is fine, and so is generating boilerplate. Just make sure you understand everything it gave you and everything it built. It was never about whether you used AI, it's about whether you understand the result. Plenty of AI/ML communities already discuss AI-assisted programming, so we'll leave that to them and stick to the fundamentals.

In short, AI is a tool that helps us learn and work, and not something to depend on entirely.

2. Autodidacticism

Autodidacticism is a fancy word for self-learning, which means finding your own resources, experimenting, and building your own understanding instead of waiting for someone to teach you. It doesn't mean learning alone, though. Ask questions, discuss, and learn from others, but the responsibility for your learning stays with you.

This is the core of Nira. We want people who enjoy figuring things out, and we want everyone to build that habit.

So before you ask a question, do some research. Search for it, read the documentation, look through resources, and try things yourself. Still stuck? Then ask. There's nothing wrong with that.

Not sure where to start, or is the problem simply hard? That's fine too. Just explain what you're trying to do, what you've already tried, and where you got stuck. That gives people enough context to actually help.

Also, nobody here is a mentor or a teacher. Everyone is a learner, and some of us just happen to know more about certain topics than others. We share what we know, but no one is here to solve your problems for you.

What we'd like to avoid are blank questions with no context and no effort, where someone else is expected to do all the work.

It pays off in the long run, too. Knowing how to search, read documentation, and experiment is a big part of becoming a better programmer.

3. No Referrals

Nira is a learning community, so we don't offer career assistance. That means no job referrals, no recommending members for positions, and no referring anyone to a company on Nira's behalf. Being a member or a contributor doesn't entitle you to any of that.

We don't hire through the community either. The people working on Nira and its projects are here to learn, teach, experiment, and build something together. Nira isn't a company, and its members aren't employees.

Contributions are voluntary. Whether you help someone, write documentation, contribute code, organize something, or work on a community project, please don't expect to be paid for it. Nira is a non-profit, and we contribute to learn, share knowledge, and help the community grow.

That doesn't mean Nira can't help your career. What you learn, the projects you build, and the people you meet can all be useful, and you're free to use them however you like. But that's a side effect of taking part, and not a service we provide or a promise we make.

We're also not a bootcamp, a certification platform, or a placement program. Contributing won't earn you a certificate, a referral, a title, or a reward, and we'd rather not have people join just to collect them. If that's what you're after, Nira is probably not the right place, and that's completely fine. Plenty of other communities focus on exactly that.

To us, Nira is simply a place to learn and build things together. Whatever career benefits come out of that are yours to take, but we don't want to turn learning into a transaction.
